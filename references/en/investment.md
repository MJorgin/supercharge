# Business and Investment Analysis

English companion to [the Chinese guide](../investment.md), which contains source links. These are public frameworks and metrics, not an institution's decision on a specific investment. Verify current facts, definitions and professional requirements; never invent missing data.

## Sequoia business-plan outline
- **Use:** Present or evaluate the core logic of a venture.
- **Do:** Summarize purpose, problem, solution, why now, market, competition, business model, team, financials and vision; label evidence and unknowns.
- **Limits:** This is Sequoia's public writing guide, not its complete investment process; a filled outline is not investment approval.

## Why Now
- **Use:** Explain why an opportunity may be feasible now rather than earlier.
- **Do:** Identify verifiable shifts in technology, cost, distribution, regulation or behavior; explain the barrier changed and whether competitors benefit too.
- **Limits:** Separate observed changes from trends and forecasts; a window does not guarantee opportunity.

## Bottom-up market sizing
- **Use:** Estimate the potential size of a reachable market.
- **Do:** Define geography, people and timeframe; multiply checkable inputs such as addressable customers, feasible usage and price; give scenarios and check units and overlap.
- **Limits:** Potential is not realized revenue; show assumptions and ranges and do not substitute total industry size for reachable demand.

## Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
- **Use:** Understand acquisition spending per new customer and channel efficiency.
- **Do:** Divide relevant sales and marketing costs by corresponding new customers for a defined period and channel; compare paid and blended CAC and note attribution and timing.
- **Limits:** Do not compare inconsistent definitions or mix free and paying users; spending and conversion can occur in different periods.

## Lifetime value (LTV)
- **Use:** Estimate economic value generated by customer segments over their lifetime.
- **Do:** State revenue, variable cost, retention and discount assumptions; prefer observed cohort contribution profit, with scenario and sensitivity analysis as needed.
- **Limits:** Revenue is not profit; 1/churn assumes conditions such as stable churn and can be misleading with short or changing data.

## CAC payback
- **Use:** Estimate when acquired customers' margin recovers acquisition spending.
- **Do:** Align cohorts and acquisition costs; calculate time for cumulative gross or contribution margin to cover costs, with assumptions.
- **Limits:** Revenue payback is not profit payback; sales cycles, one-time costs and variable costs matter, and SaaS benchmarks may not transfer.

## Gross and net revenue retention (GRR / NRR)
- **Use:** Distinguish churn and contraction from expansion in subscription revenue.
- **Do:** Fix the starting customer cohort and period; GRR subtracts churn and contraction, while NRR also includes expansion from that cohort.
- **Limits:** Exclude new-customer revenue from the starting cohort; revenue retention differs from user retention and definitions vary.

## GMV versus platform revenue
- **Use:** Separate transaction volume from what a marketplace earns.
- **Do:** State GMV, platform revenue, refunds/cancellations and costs separately; assess unit economics using revenue and costs.
- **Limits:** GMV is neither revenue nor profit; accounting conventions can vary.

## Cash burn and runway
- **Use:** Evaluate cash pressure in an operating plan.
- **Do:** Forecast opening cash and periodic net cash flows under scenarios, accounting for financing assumptions and monthly cash gaps.
- **Limits:** Cash divided by current burn is only a rough estimate under stable conditions; seasonality, debt and payment timing matter.
