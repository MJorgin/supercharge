# Growth and User Analysis

English companion to [the Chinese guide](../growth.md), which includes source links. Define populations, denominators, time windows and uncertainty; user journeys are not always linear.

## AARRR / AAARRR
- **Use:** Identify obstacles to acquisition, activation, retention and monetization.
- **Do:** AARRR covers Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral and Revenue; AAARRR is a variant adding Awareness. Define events, users and windows for each relevant stage, then investigate drop-off and test improvements.
- **Limits:** Order and relevance depend on the business; awareness is not acquisition, impressions are not unique users, and a funnel alone does not prove causes.

## Conversion funnel
- **Use:** Locate drop-off across a multistep journey.
- **Do:** Define the population, event sequence, observation window and deduplication; compare conversion by channel, device or cohort and formulate testable hypotheses.
- **Limits:** Missing events and multiple paths distort results; a low rate does not by itself identify the root cause.

## Cohort retention
- **Use:** Compare sustained use by acquisition or payment cohort.
- **Do:** Group users by a defined start event; specify retention behavior, window and denominator; compare equally aged cohorts and account for channel and seasonality.
- **Limits:** Different cohort composition can confound trends; correlation does not establish causality or network effects.

## Core-action retention
- **Use:** Logins or app opens do not adequately represent value.
- **Do:** Define a value-linked action, such as completed bookings, and measure its repetition across cohorts against business outcomes.
- **Limits:** Activity is a proxy and does not alone prove better experience or revenue.

## North Star and input metrics
- **Use:** Align scattered product metrics around customer value.
- **Do:** Define an outcome reflecting value and long-term business health, choose actionable inputs, test their relationships and maintain quality or risk guardrails.
- **Limits:** One metric cannot capture every goal; a metric tree is not proof of causality.

## Product-market fit disappointment survey
- **Use:** Explore how much defined active users depend on a product.
- **Do:** Ask real users how disappointed they would be if it disappeared; segment responses and reasons, and examine retention, payments and behavior.
- **Limits:** The 40% benchmark in the Superhuman case is not a universal threshold; samples and selection affect results and a score alone cannot prove fit.

## Marketplace liquidity and network effects
- **Use:** Assess matching of supply and demand in a marketplace.
- **Do:** Measure matching, waiting, transactions and core-action retention by location and group; compare cohorts and formulate testable network-effect hypotheses.
- **Limits:** Growth or retention alone does not prove network effects; geography, category and supply-demand balance matter.

## Manually serve early users
- **Use:** Learn needs before a repeatable delivery model is established.
- **Do:** Recruit a few target users, help them complete tasks manually and record behavior and obstacles; productize repeated needs when justified.
- **Limits:** Enthusiastic individuals do not represent a market; track service costs and do not assume manual work scales.
