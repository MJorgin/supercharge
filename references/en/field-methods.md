# Research, Strategy and Collaboration

English companion to [the Chinese guide](../field-methods.md), where references are linked. Cross-domain applications require checking structural similarities, differences and assumptions.

## Investigation: encounter the facts
- **Use:** User behavior, operational processes or real conditions are unclear.
- **Do:** Define the questions; inspect available records and processes, talk to relevant people, seek counterexamples and distinguish observation, testimony, inference and gaps.
- **Limits:** Small interviews may not represent the population. Respect privacy and permissions; do not claim fieldwork without doing it.

## Identify the current critical problem
- **Use:** Interconnected issues compete for limited resources.
- **Do:** Compare constraints by their effect on the goal, other problems and feasible action; identify priorities, evidence, parallel necessities and a review point.
- **Limits:** More than one issue may be critical; priorities change and safety requirements still apply.

## Analyze context: similarities and conditions
- **Use:** Adapt a practice from another team, industry or case.
- **Do:** Identify its assumptions, compare goals, resources, users and constraints, adapt the approach and test it on a suitable scale.
- **Limits:** Similar cases do not guarantee similar results; context does not justify ignoring established knowledge.

## Study cases and scale in stages
- **Use:** Understand a complex mechanism or trial an unproven approach.
- **Do:** Select informative cases and counterexamples, document context and results, derive testable hypotheses and validate across different settings before scaling.
- **Limits:** Cases and pilots may not generalize; note selection bias and special support. Case study and pilot rollout are separable steps.

## Enter a niche and expand
- **Use:** Resources are limited and the main market is difficult to enter.
- **Do:** Identify a reachable segment, compare demand and costs, test value and sustainability, then examine whether capabilities and channels transfer to adjacent segments.
- **Limits:** A niche can be too small or expensive; success there does not guarantee wider expansion.

## Small steps and feedback
- **Use:** Answers must develop through action and observation.
- **Do:** State a hypothesis, take an appropriate low-cost action, record expectations and observations, then adapt, expand or stop.
- **Limits:** Similar to PDCA and minimum viable testing; one local success is not a general rule. Avoid reckless trials when consequences are irreversible.

## After-action review (AAR)
- **Use:** Learn from a completed project, event or decision.
- **Do:** Compare intentions and actual events, examine evidence for possible causes, and assign concrete changes and follow-up.
- **Limits:** Avoid hindsight bias and blame; unverified causes remain hypotheses.

## DACI decision roles
- **Use:** Participants are unclear about who drives, decides, contributes or is informed.
- **Do:** Identify a Driver, Approver, Contributors and Informed parties, along with scope, timing and evidence.
- **Limits:** Roles must match real authority; AI cannot grant it. A full framework is unnecessary for trivial decisions.

## Working agreements
- **Use:** A team repeatedly struggles with communication, handoffs or collaboration.
- **Do:** Identify problems together and agree on channels, response expectations, handoffs, escalation and review dates.
- **Limits:** A draft is not team consensus; adapt to roles, people and time zones.

## Control chart
- **Use:** Distinguish ordinary process variation from signals worth investigating over time.
- **Do:** Select a suitable chart, collect chronological data, calculate control limits with an appropriate method and investigate signals.
- **Limits:** Control limits differ from targets and specification limits; sparse or unsuitable data cannot establish loss of control.
