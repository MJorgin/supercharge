# Game Theory, Negotiation and Strategic Interaction

English companion to [the Chinese guide](../game-theory.md), which includes sources. Models clarify interdependent choices but do not guarantee predictions. State players, options, information, timing, payoffs and constraints; treat unknown intentions and payoffs as assumptions.

## Game models and payoff matrices
- **Use:** Outcomes depend on the interacting choices of multiple parties.
- **Do:** Identify players, strategies, timing, information and preferences; construct a manageable payoff matrix and compare outcomes.
- **Limits:** Payoffs may only be qualitative or ranges; omitted choices and institutions can change results.

## Dominant and dominated strategies
- **Use:** Compare a player's options across the other party's choices.
- **Do:** Compare payoffs by scenario, distinguish strict from weak dominance and identify strictly dominated options.
- **Limits:** Many situations have no dominant strategy; conclusions depend on payoffs.

## Best responses and Nash equilibrium
- **Use:** Examine potentially stable combinations of strategies.
- **Do:** Find each party's best responses and combinations where no party benefits from changing unilaterally.
- **Limits:** Equilibria may be multiple or mixed; stability does not mean fairness, optimality or that an outcome will occur.

## Prisoner's dilemma
- **Use:** Examine whether individual short-term incentives can leave both parties worse off.
- **Do:** State and verify the payoff ranking, then consider how monitoring, trust, repeated interaction or institutions might alter incentives.
- **Limits:** Not every conflict is a prisoner's dilemma; do not presume defection or payoff order.

## Coordination games
- **Use:** Parties want to coordinate but are uncertain about a standard, time or solution.
- **Do:** Map possible coordinated outcomes and preferences; consider signals, communication, defaults and switching costs.
- **Limits:** Multiple equilibria and distributional conflicts may remain; actual coordination requires participation.

## Repeated games and cooperation
- **Use:** Ongoing relationships make present choices affect future interactions.
- **Do:** Consider frequency, observability, errors and exit, weighing short-term gains against long-term value; design transparent feedback and repair.
- **Limits:** Repetition does not guarantee cooperation; finite horizons, noise and power differences matter.

## Backward induction
- **Use:** Analyze a finite sequential game where later choices respond to earlier actions.
- **Do:** Draw the sequence, information and branches; reason backward from later best responses to compare initial actions.
- **Limits:** This is game analysis, not ordinary backward project planning; results depend on payoffs, observability and behavioral assumptions.

## Credible commitment
- **Use:** Others' beliefs about a future action affect present decisions.
- **Do:** Compare the stated action with future incentives, capacity, enforceability and exit costs; consider verifiable, feasible commitments.
- **Limits:** An unsupported promise may not be credible; first movers do not always benefit and commitment can reduce flexibility.

## Information asymmetry and signaling
- **Use:** Parties know different things about quality, ability or intent.
- **Do:** Identify who knows what and what requires verification; compare evidence, third-party checks and trials as possible signals.
- **Limits:** Signals are not necessarily true; never fabricate credentials, testimonials or guarantees.

## Screening and due diligence
- **Use:** Choose partners, suppliers or projects when relevant information is unevenly distributed.
- **Do:** Define relevant questions, supporting documentation and trial criteria; cross-check and record unresolved items.
- **Limits:** Cost, privacy and fairness must be proportionate to risk; do not confuse bias with evidence.

## Principal-agent incentives
- **Use:** A principal delegates to an agent with different goals, information or exposure to risk.
- **Do:** Map goals, observable actions and outcomes; assess incentive distortions and design metrics, safeguards, feedback and responsibilities.
- **Limits:** Not all motivation is financial; optimizing a single measure can encourage gaming or harm long-term value.

## BATNA
- **Use:** Evaluate the best feasible alternative if negotiation fails.
- **Do:** List workable no-deal options, assess cost, timing and risk, choose the best and update as information changes; use it to inform a reservation point.
- **Limits:** A BATNA is an action plan, not a quoted price; speculative options are not established alternatives.

## ZOPA
- **Use:** Assess whether an agreement can be preferable to each party's no-deal alternative.
- **Do:** Define your own reservation point, estimate the other party's based on evidence and assess overlap across price, timing and service.
- **Limits:** The other party's threshold is usually unknown; multi-issue trades may create value even without overlap on one price.

## Interests, positions and multi-issue trades
- **Use:** Stated positions conflict but parties may value terms differently.
- **Do:** Separate demands from underlying needs and explore combinations of price, timing, quality and other terms.
- **Limits:** Verify others' interests rather than asserting them; do not conceal material facts or claim authority that has not been granted.
